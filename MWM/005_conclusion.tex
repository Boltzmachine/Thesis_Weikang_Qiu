\section{Conclusion}

In this work, we introduced the Mind World Model (\mwm{}) for learning from synchronized EEG and egocentric video in EgoBrain. \mwm{} treats EEG as the latent action of a predictive visual world model. Its Lateralization-Aware Transformer separates bilateral from side-specific activity and couples this structure to paired EEG--video reflections. The resulting mind model uses only EEG at inference time. In the reported linear-probe comparisons, it leads all \mwmClsWins{} classification tasks and all \mwmRegWins{} regression tasks. Relative to the strongest single foundation-model baseline, it improves mean balanced accuracy by \mwmClsGainPct{}\% and mean pooled Pearson correlation by \mwmRegGainPct{}\%.

We retain the world model as a complementary output and fit a lightweight adapter from independently measured IMU signals to its frozen action interface. Relative to the inverse-dynamics latent-action baseline, it reduces future-feature L1 error by \mwmWMWithinGainPct{}\% within recordings and \mwmWMSubjectGainPct{}\% on held-out subjects. Together, these results support synchronized neural and visual experience as a route to representations that transfer both to motor decoding and to action-conditioned prediction.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} Although the positive action gap indicates that the predictor uses the mapped actions, further evaluation is needed before interpreting the interface as counterfactual control.

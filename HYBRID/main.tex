\chapter{Learning High-Order Relationships of Brain Regions}
\label{chapter:hybrid}

Chapter~\ref{chapter:repqa} examined how a language-based interface balances readability and informativeness. We now turn to the neural signals that could support a more direct interface between people and machines. A first question is how to discover useful neural priors, such as structural relationships, that improve brain signal modeling and predictive performance. This chapter introduces \hybrid{}, which learns high-order relationships among brain regions for predicting cognitive outcomes from fMRI. By identifying informative groups of regions and estimating their strengths, the approach provides a structural starting point for the subsequent study of what brain activity conveys about the world.
